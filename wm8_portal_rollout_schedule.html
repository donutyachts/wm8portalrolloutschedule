Fixed constraints (not adjustable here)
  • Tuesday is the base cadence — no Mondays, Fridays, weekends, or Canadian/Ukrainian holidays. Some batches also land on a Thursday under the compressed cadence below; Tuesday and Thursday are the only two days ever used.
  • Compressed cadence for specific batches (alestra, rogersrdc, windstream, default): once one of these rolls out, the next batch alternates Tue→Thu (+2 days)→Tue (+5 days) instead of a full week, to fit more value in before the freeze.
  • 3 reserved empty slots right after the Aplus batch — held open intentionally, no HIDs assigned, and excluded from sequence numbering entirely
  • Batches 1–4 capped at 5 HIDs each while the Portal process is validated capped — reserved slots don't count toward this budget, only real batches do
  • Batch 5 onward: cap lifted — each remaining custom_name group or infra batch rolls out as one uncapped batch, WM8 and Portal together uncapped. Clusters are always uncapped regardless of position (see "How this sequence was built").
  • 3-day lead time for all custom_name batches (retail, the discovered branding groups, and internal default/hostpapadefault); 7-day lead time for clusters — required for both the WM8 and Portal teams before each rollout day
  • 30-day advance notice to partners ahead of every batch's rollout date, regardless of batch type
  • Each batch always starts on a fresh day — no mixing leftover slots across group boundaries
  • Monitoring runs the days immediately following each rollout, up to the next scheduled batch — no separate week is blocked out for it
  • Code freeze: as currently sequenced, the last 2026 batch is c0 on Tue Dec 8, 2026; nothing rolls out again until c7 on Tue Jan 12, 2027. Covers the official freeze window of Fri Dec 11, 2026 – Fri Jan 8, 2027 freeze — shown as its own set of weekly rows in the schedule. These specific dates shift if the start date changes; the freeze window itself (Dec 11 – Jan 8) does not.
How this sequence was built
Rules are listed in priority order — each one applies only after everything above it has been satisfied. 18 real batches cover 308 HIDs in total.
  1. Priority 1 — Scope is filtered first, before any ordering happens: 8 HIDs already rolled out elsewhere are dropped entirely; the digitalspaceforfive group (5 HIDs) is suspended and excluded from all batches; 5 high-volume or separate-infrastructure HIDs are pulled into a 2027 batch (Tbaytel, SaskTel, EarthLink, TRIARA, Telenor Norway). Two clusters (c35, c38) ended up empty once their only HIDs were removed this way, and dropped out of the schedule entirely.
  2. Priority 2 — Shared Portal branding is non-negotiable: any HIDs sharing the same custom_name in the branding sheet must roll out on the same day, full stop — this overrides everything below it. Aplus and DigitalSpace (retail) plus six more groups discovered by checking every custom_name against the cluster schedule — alestra, breatheinternet, fairpointwebservices, rogersrdc, windstream, northernteldatacorp — were pulled out of their home clusters into standalone batches. breatheinternet, windstream, and fairpointwebservices have since been manually combined into one joint batch; the others remain standalone. default and hostpapadefault are also custom_name groups (internal infrastructure ones — see Priority 3).
  3. Priority 3 — Internal infrastructure still gets upgraded, on the same 3-day lead time as any other custom_name batch: default and hostpapadefault are internal/monitoring infrastructure, not real partners, but they're still custom_name batches under Priority 2 — each gets its own dedicated batch, placed after the partner-facing branding groups.
  4. Priority 4 — Clusters are never split, same as branding groups: a cluster's entire remaining membership always rolls out as one batch on one day, no matter how many HIDs it has or what position it lands in — it stays whole and is exempt from the 5-HID cap even if positioned in one of the first 4 slots. (Retail/custom_name groups don't get this same protection — they can still be split if they land somewhere too small to hold them, as DigitalSpace originally was.) c30, c45, and c8 have since been manually combined into one joint batch.
  5. Priority 5 — Clusters ordered by fewest remaining logins first: once the above groups are pulled out, the remaining clusters are sequenced ascending by their remaining total 30-day logins (recalculated after exclusions/pulls, not the original pre-exclusion figure — several clusters lost their highest-volume HID to a branding pull or 2027 deferral, which would have broken a naive ascending sort based on stale totals).
  6. Priority 6 — Within a cluster, lowest-usage partners first: HIDs inside each remaining cluster are ordered ascending by individual 30-day login count. This has no effect on the schedule's dates, since clusters are never split (Priority 4) — it's purely cosmetic ordering for the HID list display.
  7. Priority 7 — Manual placement, layered on top of all of the above: the 3 reserved empty slots, the position of rogersrdc/northernteldatacorp/alestra ahead of DigitalSpace, the merge of breatheinternet + windstream + fairpointwebservices into one batch, the merge of c30 + c45 + c8 into one batch, the specific compressed-cadence batches, and the Seq numbering skipping reserved slots were all added by direct instruction rather than derived from the rules above. Priorities 1–4 (scope, branding grouping, and cluster atomicity) are hard rules baked into how the schedule is generated; Priorities 5–7 (cluster order, within-cluster order, manual placement) are the adjustable layer on top.
